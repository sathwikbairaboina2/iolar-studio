01

The studio

Instastudio is one Next.js application on one machine that produces five kinds of Instagram content. They are peers, not a product and its offshoots: each has its own document, its own renderer and its own page in the app, and each was built because the previous one proved the machinery underneath.

PipelineTakesMakesRendered by
Reels A topic researched into a cited script, or a script pasted whole A vertical short: voiced, shot, captioned, cut ffmpeg, plus the lipsync, webpage and templates services
Handbooks A topic or reference pages, written into a syllabus A ten-image carousel of handwritten-notes pages Headless Chromium at 2×, downscaled by sharp
Jobs reels A week of the research pool's open roles One sixty-second reel of that week The jobs-reel plugin, from a cut the studio prepares
Job cards One listing — a URL, or a row already in the pool A single posted image for that opening Headless Chromium, one of six card families
Explainers A brief: topic, audience, the one thing that must land A 14–21 second board that mutates, with no cuts in it The reels-templates service, as its second category

Stories, quote cards and memes are navigation stubs marked soon. Everything else in the sidebar is shared plumbing: the Knowledge graph the research agents read, the Assets library, the Plugins board that shows which local service is up, and Settings, which holds the one switch that decides who does the thinking.

What is actually in the library

Thirty-three reels, ten explainer boards, twenty-seven handbook chapters across thirteen subjects, and a research pool holding about sixteen hundred open roles. The numbers matter to the design: they are why lists are paged, why the knowledge graph is capped at four hundred nodes for drawing, and why coverage is judged across a whole chapter rather than a page.

02

The rules everything obeys

Four rules cut across all five pipelines. Most of the machinery in this manual exists to keep one of them, and most of the bugs worth remembering were a rule quietly broken.

1 · Content is never trimmed to fit

When material does not fit its frame, the frame grows or the layout changes — the material does not shrink. A handbook page past ten becomes part two of the chapter; a job card whose family cannot hold it renders in band-stack, where every field has its own band; a reel bite is never cut mid-sentence; an explainer panel refuses a fifth line rather than setting four in smaller type. Text is never scaled down to make something tidy.

2 · Nothing is invented

A model may arrange, word and summarise what it was given. It may not add a fact. A job card whose listing never mentioned pay says the salary is missing rather than guessing it; a reel script cites the sources the hunt actually found; a handbook page teaches the syllabus rather than the model's memory of the topic. Absence is an output, and the UI shows it.

3 · Every AI edit is a proposal

No model writes to disk. A revision returns a diff the user accepts or rejects, and the guards run on the way in: a proposal with fewer blocks, fewer pages, or a newly uncovered syllabus point is refused before anyone sees it.

4 · Local first, and degrade rather than fail

Nine services run on this machine and every one of them is optional. A service that is down removes one capability and names itself on the page that wanted it — the board is empty because the jobs-reel plugin is not answering, the graph page says Neo4j refused the connection, the polish step is skipped when ComfyUI is not there. Nothing blocks the app from opening.

Why these four and not a style guide

Each of them is mechanically enforced somewhere: block counts and page counts in the handbook guards, the mandatory-field gate in job cards, diff-approve in every AI route, and a health probe per service behind every empty state. A rule nothing checks is a preference.

03

The shared spine

All five pipelines are the same five moves. Knowing them once is most of knowing the codebase, because each pipeline is these moves with its own document and its own renderer.

The document
One Zod schema per pipeline — PostSchema, ReelSchema, JobsFile, JobCardSchema, ExplainerDoc — parsed at every boundary. A malformed generation fails at the edge instead of corrupting a chapter.
Files are the truth
Documents live on disk under data/. SQLite and Neo4j are derived indexes, rebuildable, and indexing never fails a save.
The browser is the typesetter
Handbook pages and job cards are laid out by headless Chromium at twice the target size and downscaled with Lanczos‑3, because handwriting fonts and rough.js strokes alias badly at 1080.
Measure, never estimate
Fit is read back out of the page — overflow in pixels, fill as a percentage, which blocks spilled. Every layout decision downstream is made from those numbers.
Deterministic renders
Jitter is seeded from the document, so the same input gives a byte-identical PNG. That is what makes golden-image tests possible at all.
The model is a stage, not a service
Every model call goes through thinker(); see section 11. No pipeline talks to a provider directly.
04

Pipeline: reels

A reel is a vertical short built from a script the user approved. It is the largest pipeline in the studio — lib/reel/ is thirty modules — because it owns research, writing, voice, footage, alignment, assembly and handoff to an editor.

The document

beats[]
The script as units: a role (hook, point, payoff), the line to say, and a shot brief. A freeform reel is a list of beats.
vo
The voice take: provider, file, duration, and the word timings whisper measured. A scriptHash records which script the take was made from, so an edited script shows its take as stale.
assets[]
Everything a shot might use, each with a status: queued, running, ready, failed, awaiting-manual.
timeline
Clips, caption cues and overlays — the cut itself, in seconds.
pattern
Null for a freeform reel. Set when the reel renders through a template service instead of beats.
style, ratio, fps, seed
The creator preset and the frame. 9:16 at 30fps by default.

Status is derived from the document, never stored: a reel with clips is assembled, with word timings but no clips voiced, with an exported mp4 exported, otherwise draft.

Research, with three gates

The Research tab does not simply fetch. It runs a graph — plan, hunt, critique, gather, draft — and stops at three points where a human decides: the plan before any hunting happens, the review of what was found, and the approval of the drafted script. The same graph runs on the local model or on headless Claude subagents depending on the one switch.

From script to cut

  1. Voice. Kokoro reads the script locally, or a recording is dropped in. Either way whisper measures the words, and the timings become the spine of everything after.
  2. Bites. The script is cut into spans on measured audio, never on estimated durations, and never mid-sentence.
  3. Shots. Talking-head footage comes from the lipsync service driving ComfyUI; page shots come from the webpage service recording a phone browsing the source a line cites; anything else is dropped in by hand.
  4. Assembly. Clips, captions and overlays are laid on the timeline against the word timings, so a caption appears on the word it belongs to.
  5. Export or handoff. ffmpeg writes export/reel.mp4, and the handoff also writes a Shotcut project.mlt and a Kdenlive project from one builder, so the cut can be finished by hand.

Pattern reels

A pattern reel skips beats. Its approved script is split into a template's sections, voiced from those lines, given a presenter clip, and rendered by the reels-templates service with Remotion. Stages are derived from disk: unsectioned → sectioned → voiced → aligned → presented → rendering → done. The Pattern tab drives it and embeds the service's own preview.

Two rules learned the hard way

Measure the audio before planning bites, and never cut a bite mid-sentence — a reel assembled from estimated durations drifts out of sync by the third beat. And captions are spelled from the script but timed from whisper: the script is what the reel says, the timings are when it says it.

05

Pipeline: handbooks

The oldest pipeline, and the one that taught the studio to measure. A topic or a set of reference pages becomes a syllabus; the syllabus becomes pages; the pages are typeset in a browser, checked, and packaged as a ten-image carousel.

Four levels, and why

Subject → chapter → part → page. The book and its references live at chapter level because every part of a chapter publishes that syllabus between them — which is why coverage is judged across all parts merged, never one part at a time. Instagram shows ten images, so MAX_SLIDES = 10 is a publishing constraint: an eleventh page becomes part two, never a deletion.

The grid that makes a page look drawn

A page is a packed two-column grid, not a vertical list. Every block is measured twice in the browser — at page width and at column width — and the packer decides span from that evidence. A half-width unit wins on area while its squeezed height stays under twice its full height, so the break-even is exactly 2.0 and SQUEEZE_LIMIT sits just under it at 1.95. It was guessed at 1.6 once, which wasted a column on every page.

Two bindings protect meaning: a heading travels with the block beneath it, and consecutive code blocks merge into one unit, so a worked example can never be split across columns.

What the gates catch before a human looks

runGates is deterministic and cheap, so it runs first. It reads structure, not taste: a snippet starting mid-construct, a def with no body, a table row whose cell count differs from its header, a duplicate heading, a page under 70% full, a page with any overflow at all. Snippets marked runnable are executed in an isolated temp directory with no network and a five-second limit, and their real output is compared against the # value comment claiming it — which turns a claim in the notes into something the build can falsify.

Only then does the review agent run: four Claude passes with deliberately narrow briefs — facts, visual (it reads the rendered PNGs), pedagogy, brand. The agent never edits, and a pass that cannot run says so rather than returning an empty list, because silence would look like approval.

Coverage

Coverage asks whether every point the book promised still appears on a page. Point and page text pass through the same tokenizer and stemmer, each word is weighted by how rare it is inside that book, and the point counts as taught at COVERED = 0.42 — a threshold chosen by scoring candidate matchers against the whole library on two axes: false flags on intact chapters, and real loss detected when half the pages are cut away.

06

Pipeline: jobs reels

The research service pulls open roles from a hundred-odd sources into a pool. The studio's job is to turn a week of that pool into one reel, and to make every decision on the way visible.

The pull
Pages the research pool into the ledger and diffs it against last time: added, refreshed, closed. A research service that is down is one pulls row with an error, never a thrown page.
The cut
A weekly or daily window. Rows map across by rule where a rule fires — function, experience band, work mode, rupee salary, city aliasing — and by one batched model call for the titles no rule placed. mappedBy records which, per row.
Thin cuts
A cut under the configured floor is marked thin and refuses to submit without an explicit force.
Your own voice
A recording dropped on a cut is used instead of the synthetic take; the plugin fits it to the cut's lines by whisper words.
The board
Submitted cuts appear as weeks on the plugin, which plans, scripts, voices and builds. The Jobs page shows the board's stages and links; the detail stays on the plugin.
07

Pipeline: job cards

A reel speaks for a whole week of roles. A job card speaks for one: a single opening rendered as an image ready to post. It inherits the handbooks rule in a stricter form — a card never says anything the listing did not — and enforces it with a gate before rendering rather than a repair pass afterwards.

The document and its gate

lib/jobcard/schema.ts holds JobCardSchema. Seven fields are mandatory — company, role, skill, experience, location, salary range, eligibility, and a source to point at — and a document missing any of them is stored as a draft rather than rendered. missingMandatory names the gaps in the words the page uses, so the tab asks for what is missing instead of drawing a card with a hole in it. The selection process is mandatory if available: absent from the source it is absent from the card, and the families that show it fall back to another block.

Six families, one slot vocabulary

Each family is a directory under templates/_jobcard/ holding template.html and a manifest. The leading underscore keeps them out of builtTemplates() and the carousel contract test — a job card is not a handbook page and shares none of that machinery. They share one slot vocabulary, so the same document fills any of them.

familyRatioPicked when
split-hero3:4One role, few facts, a company visual exists.
infographic4:5Several requirements and no usable image — until the facts pass a dozen, where its rows run off the card.
campaign3:4A drive or walk-in with dates and announced rounds.
bento4:5Facts short and of equal weight; one more field is one more tile.
ticket3:4One role with a deadline; the perforated shape is the scroll-stopper.
band-stack3:4Nothing may be cut. Every field gets a labelled band, so it cannot run out of room.
Fallback, not shrinkage

renderCard screenshots the picked family and measures whether the content ran past the frame. A family that could not hold the document is not given a smaller font — band-stack renders instead and the reply says fellBack, which the tab shows. This is the never-trim rule wearing different clothes.

Reading a listing that does not want to be read

Modern applicant tracking systems serve a JavaScript shell. An Ashby page is about twelve hundred characters of “you need to enable JavaScript” with the entire four-thousand-character posting inside its JobPosting markup as escaped HTML. postingText therefore prefers the markup's description whenever it is the longer text, and falls back to the page for sites that still render one. Before that fix a live read came back with company, role and city and nothing else.

The mark must be the company's

Every icon on a tracking system's page belongs to the tracking system. Taking one puts Ashby's logo on the hiring company's card — worse than no logo, because it is another company's mark presented as this one's. lib/jobcard/logo.ts looks in this order, and validates whatever it downloads:

  1. hiringOrganization.logo, if the posting publishes one.
  2. The company's own website, followed from that markup's sameAs.
  3. The job page's own icons — but only when the host is not one of some thirty tracking systems and aggregators.
  4. A search of the local SearXNG for the company's site, accepted only when the domain matches the company name.
  5. A wordmark set in heavy type, which is a real fallback and not a placeholder.

A candidate is measured before it is kept: at least 48 pixels on the short side and a shape between 3:5 and 8:5. That refuses the two things that are not marks — a 1200×630 social banner and a 16×16 favicon. The name check earns its place too: the first live search for “Snapscale” returned Microsoft's logo.

08

Pipeline: explainers

The newest pipeline, and the only one whose output has no cuts in it at all. An explainer is one scene that mutates: the board is declared once, each beat says what changes about it, and the viewer keeps a single picture in their head while values move inside it. That is what makes a before-and-after read as a comparison rather than as two unrelated screens.

Why it looks the way it does

The shape was measured, not chosen. Ten reels scraped from the reference channel run 14–21 seconds with zero scene cuts, four to six rows over three tiers, and a three or four line log panel under the board. Every constraint below is one of those numbers written down.

The document

scene
Declared once for the whole reel: a title, a subtitle, the rows, the links between them, and an optional log panel. This is the skeleton that holds position.
beats[]
Two to fourteen, each with a role and a list of mutations. A beat is not a shot — it is a statement about what is different now.
mutations
Five operations and that is the whole vocabulary: set moves a row's value or state, add brings a row in, link draws a path, text rewrites the title, subtitle or note, panel pushes a log line.
brief
Topic, audience, claim, target seconds — what the board was written from. Optional: a board built by hand has none, and the Script card says so rather than showing an empty form.
theme
One of six, three light and three dark. The explainer category has its own palettes; borrowing the tools stack's would have tied one category's look to another's.

Folding is the validation. A mutation naming a row that does not exist yet, or adding one twice, is caught when the document is parsed rather than when it renders. Roles are ordered too — exactly one hook, exactly one payoff, at most one cta, in that order — because a reel that argues its payoff before its steps is a broken document, not a stylistic choice.

Every edit is a command

The editor does not write documents — it issues commands, and a command is what makes undo possible. Eleven of them cover the board: beat role, timing, add, reorder, remove; the mutations; the scene rows. Each one lands in a history the Undo button walks back, and a model's whole rewrite arrives through the same door as doc.replace marked agent, which is one purple row in the history panel and one press of Undo to take back.

The subtle part is routing: an edit made while the first beat is selected changes the scene as it opens; the same edit on a later beat becomes a mutation on that beat. Getting that wrong does not crash — it silently writes a second set for the same row in the same beat, which folds and renders cleanly and is simply wrong. The node inspector says which it is about to do, in words, above the form.

The fit report

The same idea as handbook coverage, pointed at a different question: does this board hold together? It reports the real folded duration against the brief's target, the busiest board in rows, and warnings — a beat nothing happens on, a board too sparse to be worth the frame, a panel line the fold refuses. It is advice, not a gate: an explainer renders whatever the fit says, because the author is the one who decides.

Density, and why the tests could not see it

Read against the reference reels, the first boards were the right shape and the wrong weight. A node was a 110-pixel text pill with a small icon beside its label, and three tiers of those spread over a 1330-pixel band left a 370-pixel hole between every row. The channel draws its icons at the size of the label block, which is the whole reason its boards read as full. A diagram node now stacks a 92-pixel glyph over its label, making a card about 195 pixels.

Judge a board by reading a frame

All 119 renderer tests passed the entire time that hole was there, and none of them can see a gap. Pull stills out of a render with ffmpeg and put them beside the reference files. That comparison is what found it; nothing else did, and nothing else would have.

The shelf

A list of six text columns is the wrong shape for a thing whose entire output is a picture, so the Explainers page is a shelf of cards with the rendered board on the front of each. The poster is the frame three quarters of the way through — an explainer fills up as its beats mutate it, so frame zero is the emptiest picture the reel ever has and the worst possible thumbnail. It is written when a render lands, and taken on demand for anything rendered before posters existed.

09

The knowledge layer

The graph exists to answer one practical question before anything is written: what does this account already say? Its answer goes straight into the planner's prompt, so a new chapter spends its pages on new material and a new reel does not re-explain what a previous one covered.

It outgrew SQLite. It is now Neo4j on 3124 holding the graph, with Qdrant on 3126 holding passages and verified claims — the research agents' memory. One graph, one space per category: handbooks, reels, jobs, explainers, and an all view where they meet. Everything in it is rebuildable from data/.

Why a fifth space was worth adding

Not to draw a fourth island. Topic and Claim are the vocabulary the other spaces already merge into — a chapter COVERS a topic, a reel is ABOUT one and PROVES a claim — so an explainer about CDNs lands beside the chapter that teaches CDNs and the reel that proved the same claim, and the all view finds them without being told to.

SHOWS is the edge the explainers space contributes: one shared topic per board row, which is what the reel actually drew rather than what it said it was about. It answers a question nothing else in the graph can — have I explained this with a cache node before? A save mirrors into the graph the way a chapter save already does, so the tab is never stale until the next reindex.

Two views of the same graph

The page draws the four hundred most connected nodes, and offers them flat or deep. The flat view answers what is connected to what. The three-dimensional view answers what is this shaped like — which clusters sit together, which node a whole space hangs off, where a space is really two spaces joined by one edge. Same data, same colours, and three.js is fetched only when the deep view is first opened.

What makes the deep view readable

Repulsion is capped at a radius, or the dozens of little islands a space always has push each other to the far corners and everything becomes dust around one legible cluster. The layout is warmed up before the first frame, because a background tab is handed no animation frames and the graph would sit frozen in its starting ball. The camera frames the bulk of the graph rather than all of it, since fitting everything means fitting the one stray node every space has. And twenty-five nodes are named, chosen by how much hangs off them — four hundred labels is a fog.

One card, two tenants

The 27B occupies about sixteen of the graphics card's twenty-four gigabytes, and a lipsync render needs most of that back. Before any job goes to ComfyUI the studio asks the research ollama to drop what it holds — keep_alive: 0, which ollama honours immediately — and the model reloads on the next call half a minute later. An ollama that is down is an ollama holding no VRAM, so nothing here can block a render.

Trust nvidia-smi, not the app

ComfyUI's own status endpoint reported 23 GB free at a moment when nvidia-smi reported 842 MB: it shows its allocator's view, not the card's. Every VRAM decision in this system is made from nvidia-smi.

11

Who does the thinking

The studio had three provider settings and only two of them meant anything: research hunting read one, the reel agent read another, and every other stage — the handbook planner, the reel writer, the job card reader — was wired to the Claude CLI whatever the settings said. Meanwhile the local 27B sat in sixteen of the graphics card's twenty-four gigabytes whether or not anything was going to ask it a question.

There is now one switch. llmProvider routes every model call through thinker() in lib/ai/runner.ts; the two older keys are kept in step with it, so an existing settings file and the tests that write them still say the same thing.

The two brains are not equals, and the difference is stated rather than hidden: Claude can be given tools and watched working; the local model cannot, and quietly ignores both. A stage that genuinely needs the web says so with needsTools and fails loudly on the local provider instead of answering worse from memory.

The handshake that makes it real

The research service is a separate app with its own reasons to load the model — a watchlist run, a digest, a job classified — and it pins the 27B for an hour between them. Unloading behind its back only means it loads again, so it is told which way the switch went: POST {researchUrl}/api/llm flips its keep-alive between 60m and 0. The embedder is deliberately left alone: Claude has nothing to put in its place, and nomic costs a fraction of a gigabyte.

What the database is for

iolar.db is three things and none of them is a source of truth. It mirrors handbook parts so the library can be listed and searched; it is the jobs ledger — every pull, pool row, cut, mapped row and board reply, written before the call it describes; and it indexes job cards for the history list. The handbook mirror rebuilds with scripts/reindex.mts; the pool rebuilds by pulling again. The ledger is the one part that is only ever appended to.

Each of these cost real debugging time and is now encoded in a comment, a guard, or a test. They are the parts of the system a newcomer is most likely to break again.

A job card wearing another company's logo The listing lives on an applicant tracking system, and every icon on that page — favicon, touch icon, social card — is the tracking system's own. A mark may only come from the posting's markup, the company's own site, a non-ATS job page, or a name-checked search. lib/jobcard/logo.ts.
A read that returned company, role and city and nothing else The extractor handed the model the page, and a modern ATS page is a JavaScript shell; the posting was in the markup all along. postingText prefers the markup's description when it is the longer text.
A searched logo that belonged to Microsoft The first search result for a small company is often a big company's page. The domain has to match the company name, and a name under four letters never matches at all.
The three-dimensional graph frozen in a ball A background tab is handed no animation frames, so the force layout never ticked. Warm the layout up before the first frame; frame the camera from the settled positions rather than waiting for an engine stop that never comes.
Titles like “Page 3 : Page 3:” The template prints the number and the author's title also carried one. Strip a leading page N in buildSlideHtml.

Known, open

mergeContinuations erodes authored chapter boundaries across repeated merge / re-split cycles — ten authored chapters can settle into seven pages of titles. No content is lost; the boundary metadata is. The structural fix is for post.json to separate immutable authored chapters from derived rendered pages, which has not been done.

Testing

157 files, 1,212 tests, across all five pipelines. The suite deliberately mixes fast unit tests with slow honest ones: golden-image and job card renders launch Chromium, the sandbox tests launch Python, the reel suite runs real ffmpeg encodes, and the poster tests synthesise a clip and cut a frame out of it rather than mocking the call.

Run it with fewer workers

At full parallelism Chromium, Python and ffmpeg starve each other and about twenty files time out with nothing wrong in them. npx vitest run --maxWorkers=2 is green. A file that fails in a full run is worth re-running alone before believing it.
